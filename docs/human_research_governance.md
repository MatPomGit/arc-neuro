# Human research governance

This document complements study-specific protocols and does not replace institutional ethics requirements.

## Before collection

No human-data collection should begin until the applicable study-specific precollection gates are closed and the required institutional/ethics route, consent language, recruitment procedure, storage/access controls and withdrawal handling are documented.

A repository protocol, prepared questionnaire or functioning data-export pipeline does not imply ethics approval or recruitment readiness.

## Consent and minimization

Collect only data required by the protocol. Consent materials should describe the data collected, intended uses, retention, withdrawal handling, foreseeable risks and any sharing/reuse consistent with institutional requirements.

## Privacy boundary

Direct identifiers/contact mappings remain separated from research payloads. Public Git history must not contain participant-level raw records, direct identifiers or identifying free text.

## Pilot versus confirmatory use

Cognitive interviews support comprehension and item revision. Feasibility/ESM pilots support procedure and measurement development. They must not silently become confirmatory evidence or be pooled with confirmatory data unless prospectively specified.

## Adverse or sensitive information

Study procedures should define how researchers handle participant distress, unexpected sensitive disclosures and withdrawal without improvising outcome-dependent rules after collection starts.

## Release

Human-data release requires compatibility with consent, institutional governance and de-identification. When raw data cannot be shared, publish the strongest safe reproducibility package possible: schemas, synthetic fixtures, analysis code, metadata and aggregate/derived artifacts permitted for release.
